HEART ATTACK WARNING SIGNS (AHA 2023):
Call 911 IMMEDIATELY for any of these:

CLASSIC SYMPTOMS:
- Chest discomfort: most heart attacks involve discomfort in the center of the chest that lasts more than a few minutes, or goes away and comes back. Can feel like uncomfortable pressure, squeezing, fullness or pain.
- Upper body discomfort: discomfort in one or both arms, the back, neck, jaw or stomach
- Shortness of breath: with or without chest discomfort
- Cold sweat, nausea, or lightheadedness

WOMEN'S ATYPICAL SYMPTOMS:
Women are more likely to experience: shortness of breath, nausea/vomiting, back or jaw pain
Women may have heart attacks WITHOUT chest pain

ASPIRIN GUIDANCE (AHA):
- If cardiac event suspected and patient NOT allergic to aspirin: chew (do not swallow) one regular aspirin (325mg) while waiting for 911
- Do NOT give aspirin if patient is allergic, has active GI bleeding, or takes blood thinners
- Do NOT give ibuprofen or naproxen instead — only aspirin

STROKE WARNING SIGNS — FAST (AHA 2023):
F — Face drooping: Does one side of the face droop or is it numb? Ask the person to smile. Is the person's smile uneven or lopsided?
A — Arm weakness: Is one arm weak or numb? Ask the person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward?
S — Speech difficulty: Is speech slurred? Is the person unable to speak or hard to understand? Ask the person to repeat a simple sentence.
T — Time to call 911: If the person shows ANY of these symptoms, call 911 immediately. Note the time when symptoms first appeared.

ADDITIONAL STROKE SYMPTOMS:
- Sudden numbness or weakness of face, arm, or leg, especially on one side of the body
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking or understanding speech
- Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes
- Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance or coordination
- Sudden severe headache with no known cause (thunderclap headache)

CRITICAL: Do NOT give aspirin for suspected stroke (unlike heart attack) — hemorrhagic stroke will be worsened
TIME IS BRAIN: Brain loses 1.9 million neurons every minute during stroke. Every minute of delay matters.
