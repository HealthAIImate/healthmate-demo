SOURCE: ACP Clinical Practice Guideline: Noninvasive Treatments for Acute, Subacute, and Chronic Low Back Pain 2022
AUTHORITY: American College of Physicians (ACP)
URL: https://www.acponline.org/clinical-information/guidelines
SPECIALTY: musculoskeletal
CONDITION: back pain, lower back pain, lumbar pain, muscle strain, back injury
VERSION: 2022
CMO_APPROVED: PENDING

CLASSIFICATION:
- Acute: < 4 weeks duration
- Subacute: 4-12 weeks duration
- Chronic: > 12 weeks duration
- 90% of acute low back pain resolves within 4-6 weeks with conservative treatment

FIRST-LINE OTC TREATMENT (ACP Grade A recommendation):
NSAIDs (preferred first-line for most patients):
- Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin): 400-600mg every 6-8 hours with food. Max 1200mg/day OTC. Max 10 days OTC.
  Always take with food or milk. Do not take on empty stomach.
  Avoid if: kidney disease, GI ulcer, blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin), heart failure
- Naproxen sodium (Aleve): 220mg every 8-12 hours with food. Max 440mg/day OTC. Max 10 days OTC.

Acetaminophen (second-line, less effective for back pain than NSAIDs):
- 500-1000mg every 6 hours. Max 3000mg/day. Do not exceed.

Heat therapy (strong evidence for muscle spasm):
- Heating pad or warm compress: 20 minutes, 3 times daily
- Evidence: equally effective to ibuprofen for acute muscle-related back pain
- Do not apply heat while sleeping

Non-pharmacologic (ACP strong recommendation):
- Superficial heat (evidence Level A)
- Massage therapy
- Spinal manipulation (chiropractic)
- Stay active: maintain normal activities as tolerated. DO NOT rest in bed.
- Return to normal activities: evidence shows bed rest worsens outcomes

CAUDA EQUINA SYNDROME — RED FLAG EMERGENCY (CALL 911):
- Back pain + bladder incontinence or retention (cannot urinate OR cannot control urination)
- Back pain + bowel incontinence
- Saddle numbness (numbness in groin, inner thighs, buttocks)
- Progressive leg weakness or foot drop
- These symptoms require IMMEDIATE surgical evaluation. Time-sensitive emergency.

OTHER RED FLAGS REQUIRING PHYSICIAN EVALUATION:
- Pain after significant trauma (fall, accident): possible fracture — physician or ER
- History of cancer + new back pain: possible metastasis — physician same week
- Unexplained weight loss + back pain: physician same week
- Night pain that wakes from sleep: physician evaluation
- Age > 70 + acute back pain (osteoporosis fracture risk): physician evaluation
- Intravenous drug use + back pain: possible spinal infection — ER
- Immunosuppression + back pain: physician same day
- Fever + back pain: possible kidney infection or spinal infection — physician today
- Neurological symptoms:
  - Leg weakness: physician same day
  - Foot drop: physician same day
  - Progressive numbness in legs: physician same day
  - Loss of sensation in saddle area: 911 IMMEDIATELY (cauda equina)

IMAGING:
- NOT recommended for acute back pain < 6 weeks WITHOUT red flags
- MRI/CT indicated: red flags, neurological symptoms, failure to improve at 6 weeks
- X-ray indicated: trauma, age > 70, suspected fracture

OTTAWA ANKLE RULES (for ankle injuries):
X-ray required if:
1. Bone tenderness at tip or posterior edge of lateral malleolus (fibula end)
2. Bone tenderness at tip or posterior edge of medial malleolus (tibia end)
3. Inability to bear weight (take 4 steps) immediately after injury and in clinic
If NONE of these: X-ray not needed. Conservative management with PRICE protocol.

PRICE PROTOCOL FOR SPRAINS:
- P = Protection: avoid activities causing pain for 1-3 days
- R = Rest: relative rest, not complete immobilization
- I = Ice: 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours for first 48-72 hours. Never apply ice directly to skin.
- C = Compression: elastic bandage from foot to mid-calf, firm not tight
- E = Elevation: ankle above heart level when resting
