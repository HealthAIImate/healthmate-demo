INFLUENZA SYMPTOMS:
- Fever (often high, 100-104°F), sometimes absent especially in elderly
- Cough (usually dry)
- Sore throat
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Muscle or body aches (often severe)
- Headaches
- Fatigue (can be extreme)
- Vomiting and diarrhea (more common in children)
- Onset: ABRUPT — symptoms come on suddenly, distinguishing from cold

COMMON COLD SYMPTOMS (vs Flu):
- Gradual onset over 1-3 days
- Runny/stuffy nose prominent
- Sneezing
- Mild sore throat
- Mild cough
- Low-grade or no fever
- Mild fatigue
- No severe muscle aches
- Self-limiting: 7-10 days

CENTOR CRITERIA FOR STREPTOCOCCAL PHARYNGITIS (Strep Throat):
Score 1 point each:
1. Tonsillar exudates (white/yellow patches on tonsils)
2. Tender anterior cervical lymphadenopathy (swollen glands in neck)
3. Fever (temperature > 100.4°F / 38°C)
4. Absence of cough (cough argues against strep)

Score 0-1: Antibiotic NOT recommended. Viral etiology likely.
Score 2-3: Consider rapid strep test. Antibiotic only if test positive.
Score 4: High probability strep. Rapid test or treat empirically.
NOTE: Antibiotics for viral sore throat do NOT help and cause antibiotic resistance.

OTC MANAGEMENT:
Fever and pain (adults):
- Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin): 200-400mg every 6-8 hours with food. Max 1200mg/day OTC. Max 10 days.
  Do not take with other NSAIDs. Avoid if kidney disease, GI ulcer, or blood thinners.
- Acetaminophen (Tylenol): 325-650mg every 4-6 hours. Max 3000mg/day (regular adults), 4000mg/day (healthy adults).
  Do not exceed dose. Risk of liver damage with alcohol or overdose.

Nasal congestion:
- Pseudoephedrine (Sudafed original): 30-60mg every 4-6 hours. Max 240mg/day.
  Requires ID at pharmacy counter (federal law). Avoid with hypertension, heart disease, MAOIs.
- Oxymetazoline nasal spray (Afrin): 2-3 sprays per nostril twice daily. MAX 3 days use only.
  Rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa) if used longer than 3 days.

Sore throat:
- Benzocaine lozenges (Cepacol, Chloraseptic): follow package dosing
- Saltwater gargle: 1/4-1/2 teaspoon salt in 8oz warm water, gargle and spit
- Honey: 1 tablespoon for adults (evidence-based for cough suppression). Never give to infants under 1 year.

Cough:
- Dextromethorphan (DayQuil, Robitussin DM): suppresses cough. Follow label dosing.
- Guaifenesin (Mucinex): expectorant — loosens mucus. 400-800mg every 4-12 hours with full glass of water.

RED FLAGS REQUIRING EMERGENCY EVALUATION:
- Stridor (high-pitched breathing sound): possible epiglottitis — CALL 911
- Drooling + inability to swallow: possible epiglottitis or peritonsillar abscess — CALL 911
- Muffled "hot potato" voice: peritonsillar abscess — ER same day
- Difficulty opening mouth (trismus): peritonsillar abscess — ER same day
- Respiratory distress: ER immediately
- Rash with fever (consider strep, measles): physician same day
- Symptoms > 10 days without improvement: physician evaluation
- Fever > 103°F (39.4°C) persistent for more than 3 days: physician evaluation

WHEN ANTIBIOTICS ARE APPROPRIATE:
- Confirmed strep throat (rapid test or culture positive): amoxicillin or penicillin
- Confirmed bacterial sinusitis (symptoms > 10 days, severe, or worsening after initial improvement)
- Confirmed bacterial pneumonia
NOT appropriate: viral URI, viral sore throat, viral pharyngitis (these do NOT improve with antibiotics)

PEDIATRIC CONSIDERATIONS:
- Children under 4: no OTC cold medicines (safety concerns)
- Children 4-11: use children's formulations only, weight-based dosing
- Acetaminophen: 10-15mg/kg per dose every 4-6 hours. Never exceed 5 doses in 24 hours.
- Ibuprofen (age ≥ 6 months): 10mg/kg per dose every 6-8 hours
- NEVER give aspirin to children (Reye syndrome risk)
