DEFINITION OF UNCOMPLICATED UTI:
Uncomplicated cystitis: acute cystitis occurring in a non-pregnant premenopausal woman with no known structural or functional urinary tract abnormalities or comorbidities.

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (clinical diagnosis acceptable):
- Dysuria (burning/pain with urination)
- Frequency (urge to urinate more often than normal)
- Urgency (sudden compelling urge to urinate)
- Suprapubic discomfort
- Absence of vaginal discharge or irritation
- Absence of fever (temperature < 100.4°F / 38°C)
- Absence of flank pain or costovertebral angle tenderness

SYMPTOMS THAT RULE OUT UNCOMPLICATED UTI (physician evaluation required):
- Fever > 100.4°F (38°C) with urinary symptoms: possible pyelonephritis
- Flank or back pain with urinary symptoms: possible pyelonephritis
- Nausea or vomiting with urinary symptoms: possible pyelonephritis
- Rigors (shaking chills): systemic infection
- Pregnancy: ALL UTI in pregnancy requires physician evaluation
- Men: all male UTI is considered complicated — physician required
- Children under 18: physician evaluation required
- Recurrent UTI (> 2 per year): physician evaluation required
- Structural urinary abnormalities: physician evaluation required
- Immunocompromised patients: physician evaluation required
- Symptoms > 7 days without treatment: physician evaluation required

OTC MANAGEMENT (symptomatic relief only — does NOT treat infection):
Phenazopyridine (AZO Standard, Uristat):
- Dose: 200mg (2 tablets of 100mg) OR 97.2mg per AZO product label
- Per AZO Standard label: 2 tablets 3 times daily with or after meals
- Maximum duration: 2 days OTC. After 2 days physician evaluation required
- Mechanism: urinary analgesic — relieves burning, urgency, frequency symptoms only
- Does NOT treat bacterial infection — antibiotic required for cure
- Side effects: turns urine and sometimes tears/contact lenses orange — this is normal and expected
- Contraindications: kidney disease, severe liver disease, G6PD deficiency
- Warning: if symptoms not improved after 2 days OTC use, physician evaluation required

ANTIBIOTIC TREATMENT (prescription only — IDSA first-line):
- Nitrofurantoin (Macrobid): 100mg twice daily x 5 days (preferred first-line)
- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim): if local resistance <20%, x 3 days
- Fosfomycin: 3g single dose
NOTE: HealthMate does NOT prescribe antibiotics. Physician evaluation required.

PYELONEPHRITIS RED FLAGS — GO TO ER:
- Fever > 100.4°F / 38°C + urinary symptoms
- Flank pain (pain in side or back, below ribs)
- Nausea or vomiting + urinary symptoms
- Rigors or shaking chills
- Altered mental status + urinary symptoms

SPECIAL POPULATIONS:
- Pregnancy: ANY urinary symptoms in pregnancy → physician same day, not urgent care
  Risk: untreated UTI in pregnancy → pyelonephritis in 20-30% → preterm labor risk
  No OTC phenazopyridine in pregnancy (no established safety data)
- Elderly women: may have atypical symptoms — confusion, falls, general malaise
  Lower threshold for physician evaluation in patients > 65

FOLLOW-UP: Test of cure not routinely recommended for uncomplicated UTI in young women.
Recurrent UTI (2+ per year): discuss prophylaxis with physician.
