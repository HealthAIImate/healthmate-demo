ABCDE WARNING SIGNS OF MELANOMA:
The ABCDEs of melanoma are the warning signs that a mole or pigmented spot may be melanoma.

A — Asymmetry: One half does not match the other half. A normal mole is symmetrical. If you draw a line through the middle, the two halves match.

B — Border: The edges of an early melanoma tend to be uneven. The borders may be ragged, notched, or blurred in an unusual way. The pigment may spread into surrounding skin.

C — Color: A variety of colors is a warning signal. Normal moles are a single shade of brown. A mole that shows different shades of brown, tan, or black — or areas of red, white, or blue — is a warning signal.

D — Diameter: Melanomas are usually larger than 6 millimeters (about 1/4 inch, the diameter of a pencil eraser) when diagnosed. But they can be smaller.

E — Evolving: Any change in size, shape, color, or any new symptom (such as bleeding, itching, or crusting) of a mole is a warning signal.

URGENT REFERRAL CRITERIA:
- Any single ABCDE criterion present: dermatologist evaluation recommended
- Multiple ABCDE criteria present: URGENT dermatologist referral within 1-2 weeks
- Rapid change in any characteristic: URGENT referral
- Bleeding, itching, crusting: immediate evaluation
- Family history of melanoma + suspicious lesion: URGENT

HIGH RISK POPULATIONS:
- Personal or family history of melanoma
- More than 50 moles on body
- Fair skin, light hair, light eyes
- History of severe sunburns, especially in childhood
- Outdoor occupational UV exposure (outdoor workers, lifeguards)
- Immunosuppression
- History of previous skin cancer

WHAT IS NOT MELANOMA (reassuring features):
- Perfectly symmetrical, smooth border, uniform tan/brown color
- Stable for many years without change
- Less than 6mm diameter and no other ABCDE features
- These features do NOT eliminate evaluation need if patient is concerned

OTC MANAGEMENT: No OTC treatment for suspicious lesions. Biopsy by dermatologist is the only way to confirm or rule out melanoma. Do not attempt to treat a suspicious mole with home remedies.

NEXT STEP: In-person dermatologist evaluation. Biopsy if indicated by clinical examination.

EMERGENCY RED FLAGS requiring same-day evaluation:
- Rapidly growing lesion (changed significantly in days to weeks)
- Lesion bleeding spontaneously without trauma
- Satellite lesions appearing around primary lesion
